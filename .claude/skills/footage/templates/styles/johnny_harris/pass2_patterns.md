## TIMING PARAMETERS (10 most-used animations)

*(Assumes 24 frames per second)*

1.  **Region Fill (Map Highlight)**
    *   **Duration:** 10-15 frames (0.4-0.6 seconds)
    *   **Easing Type:** Ease-out (fast start, subtle fade/slowdown)
    *   **Sync:** Independent (triggered after speech introduces the topic)

2.  **Map Zoom-in (General)**
    *   **Duration:** 24-36 frames (1-1.5 seconds)
    *   **Easing Type:** Ease-in-out (smooth acceleration and deceleration)
    *   **Sync:** Independent (often starts before or just as speech mentions the location)

3.  **Text Overlay (Direct Appearance/Disappearance)**
    *   **Duration:** Instantaneous (1-2 frames for appearance/disappearance)
    *   **Easing Type:** Linear (no visible easing, hard cut)
    *   **Sync:** Synced to speech (appears precisely when the word/concept is spoken)

4.  **Element Pop-in (Scaling from Zero to Full)**
    *   **Duration:** 10-18 frames (0.4-0.75 seconds)
    *   **Easing Type:** Ease-out (quick, energetic scale-up)
    *   **Sync:** Independent (often just after speech introduces the entity)

5.  **Map Element Draw-on (Red Border Trace)**
    *   **Duration:** 24-48 frames (1-2 seconds)
    *   **Easing Type:** Linear (steady drawing speed)
    *   **Sync:** Independent (can start slightly before or during the mention of the region)

6.  **Map Element Fill-in (From Outline to Solid Color)**
    *   **Duration:** 12-24 frames (0.5-1 second)
    *   **Easing Type:** Ease-out (quick burst of color)
    *   **Sync:** Independent (usually occurs immediately after the border is drawn or mentioned)

7.  **Object Path Animation (Curved Line)**
    *   **Duration:** 48-72 frames (2-3 seconds)
    *   **Easing Type:** Ease-in-out (smooth, arcing movement)
    *   **Sync:** Independent (often synchronized with explanatory narration about travel or connection)

8.  **Map Transition (Satellite to White/Grey Outline)**
    *   **Duration:** 12-24 frames (0.5-1 second)
    *   **Easing Type:** Linear fade
    *   **Sync:** Independent (often used at the start of a new section or when simplifying visual focus)

9.  **Map Overlay (Gradient/Heatmap-like Fill)**
    *   **Duration:** 18-30 frames (0.75-1.25 seconds)
    *   **Easing Type:** Ease-out (quick spread of color/gradient)
    *   **Sync:** Independent (accompanies narration about expansion or influence)

10. **Map Rotate/Pan (Globe Rotation)**
    *   **Duration:** 48-96 frames (2-4 seconds)
    *   **Easing Type:** Ease-in-out (smooth globe rotation)
    *   **Sync:** Independent (transitions between large geographical areas, often setting the scene for a new point)

## THE 5 SIGNATURE MOVES (Johnny Harris Style)

These are recurring stylistic choices that contribute significantly to the "Johnny Harris" feel:

1.  **The "Global Map Explainer"**:
    *   **Recipe:** Starts with a wide satellite view of the relevant region (often the Middle East), then quickly zooms in and highlights countries or areas with solid color fills (often yellow or orange) and crisp, bold text overlays. This is almost always accompanied by a fast "whoosh" sound effect and an immediate shift to a more dramatic or inquisitive musical score. Historical animations of changing borders or animated paths indicating movement/influence are frequently layered on.
    *   **Visual Specs:** High-res satellite imagery -> flat color fills (often sequential or expanding) -> sans-serif bold white text.
    *   **Audio Specs:** Fast `whoosh_fast` sound effect -> immediate shift to dramatic or investigative music.
    *   **Timing Specs:** Rapid transitions (0.5-1.5s per map change) synched with narration pace but independent of specific words.

2.  **The Direct, Conversational Talking Head with Subtle Push-in**:
    *   **Recipe:** The presenter directly addresses the camera in a casual, conversational tone, often starting with a rhetorical question or a personal observation. The camera performs a very subtle, slow push-in, making the shot feel intimate and engaging, even for a static shot. Occasional head turns or gestures are used to break monotony.
    *   **Visual Specs:** Mid-shot framing, neutral background with maps/personal items, good lighting, presenter makes direct eye contact.
    *   **Audio Specs:** Clear, present speech -> consistent ambient room tone -> underlying educational or contemplative music.
    *   **Timing Specs:** Long takes (10-30s+) with very slow, almost imperceptible push-in (ease-in-out), independent of specific speech pacing.

3.  **The Archival Footage "Flashback" Montage**:
    *   **Recipe:** Quick, often black-and-white (or vintage color) clips are rapidly cut together, showcasing historical events, people, or technologies relevant to the narrative. These clips typically have their original archival audio (speeches, sounds of war/industry) mixed in with the main music track, sometimes briefly dropping the music to emphasize the archival audio.
    *   **Visual Specs:** Variable aspect ratios, visible film grain, historical context.
    *   **Audio Specs:** Layered `archival_audio` (speech, SFX) -> dynamic music volume (often swells around segments, or drops to emphasize original audio).
    *   **Timing Specs:** Very rapid cuts (as short as 1-3 seconds per clip) synched tightly to the accompanying narration and music beat/tempo.

4.  **The "Key Concept Pop-out/Overlay"**:
    *   **Recipe:** Crucial words, phrases, or names (often in white bold text) are instantly overlaid on the screen (sometimes scaling up slightly), frequently appearing directly on top of the geographical area or person they refer to on the map. They serve to punctuate key information.
    *   **Visual Specs:** High-contrast text (e.g., white on black background, or directly on map) -> often scales up (Element Pop-in) for emphasis.
    *   **Audio Specs:** Often accompanied by a subtle `whoosh_fast` or other light impact sound, or precisely timed with the spoken word.
    *   **Timing Specs:** Instantaneous appearance (0.1-0.2s) or quick scale-up (0.4-0.75s). Tightly synchronized with speech.

5.  **The Journey/Connection Arc (Dashed Line Animation)**:
    *   **Recipe:** A dashed or solid line animates across the map, tracing a significant path, connection, or trajectory (e.g., migration, trade routes, military projection). This is almost always a curved arc, reinforcing the spherical nature of the globe.
    *   **Visual Specs:** Contrasting line color (e.g., orange or white dashed line) -> often overlaid on a satellite or simplified map.
    *   **Audio Specs:** Accompanied by a smooth `whoosh_fast` or a continuous subtle sweep sound, sometimes with a shift to more active music.
    *   **Timing Specs:** Medium to slow duration (2-4 seconds) with ease-in-out, making the movement feel purposeful. Synchronized with narration explaining the journey or connection.
